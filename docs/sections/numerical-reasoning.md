# Numerical reasoning

**In one line:** quick number problems, from number series to percentages and short word problems, that check how fast and accurately you work with numbers.

Numerical reasoning is the largest of the three content areas. PI says a candidate can attempt up to 18 numerical questions in one test, against 16 each for verbal and abstract.

## What it measures

PI describes this area as the capacity to work with numbers, interpret data and solve quantitative problems quickly and accurately. The maths itself is school level: the four operations, fractions, percentages, ratios and averages. The challenge is doing it in about 14 seconds, without mistakes, while the clock runs.

PI says the test needs no formal education or test preparation. That is true of the reasoning. But a few minutes a day on mental arithmetic and number series makes a real difference to your speed, and speed is part of what the test is built to measure.

## How the questions look

PI does not publish a list of numerical question types. The formats below are the ones we use for practice in these notes, chosen because they test the skills PI names in short, timed items.

| Format | What you do |
|---|---|
| Number series | Find the rule in a sequence and give the next number |
| Percentages | Work out a percentage, a discount or a percentage change |
| Ratios and rates | Scale a recipe, a speed or a work rate up or down |
| Averages | Find the mean of a small set of numbers |
| Comparing fractions | Decide which of several fractions or decimals is largest |
| Small tables | Read a few figures and combine them |

PI's public pages do not say whether you may use a calculator or scrap paper. Read the instructions in your invitation and on the test's first screen. Practise so that you could do every item here in your head or with a quick note, which is faster than a calculator for most of them anyway.

## A method that works

1. **Read the question line first.** Know what you are solving for (the next number, a percentage, a difference) before you read the figures.
2. **For a series, check the differences.** Write the gap between each pair of numbers. Equal gaps mean add or subtract. Growing gaps mean the step itself changes. If the gaps are messy, try multiplying, then try two series interleaved.
3. **For percentages, use friendly fractions.** 25% is a quarter, 20% is a fifth, 10% is a tenth. 30% of 140 is three tenths of 140: 14 × 3 = 42.
4. **For rates, find "one" first.** 84 forms in 7 hours is 12 an hour. Now any number of hours is one multiplication away.
5. **Estimate before you choose.** If the answer must be "a bit over 100", you can often rule out three options without finishing the sum.
6. **Check units and the question.** Did it ask for the increase or the new total? The difference, or which team won?

### Series checklist

When a sequence does not jump out, run through these in order. Most series fall to the first three.

| Check | Example |
|---|---|
| Same step | 4, 10, 16, 22 (+6) |
| Same multiplier | 3, 12, 48 (×4) |
| Step that grows or shrinks | 2, 4, 7, 11 (+2, +3, +4) |
| Two alternating steps | 20, 25, 23, 28, 26 (+5, −2) |
| Two series interleaved | 1, 20, 3, 18, 5, 16 (1, 3, 5 and 20, 18, 16) |
| Squares or cubes | 1, 8, 27, 64 (1³, 2³, 3³, 4³) |

## Traps that cost points

- **Answering a different question.** "By how much" wants the difference, not the total. "Percentage increase" divides by the starting value, not the new one.
- **Percentage change from the wrong base.** 80 to 100 is a 25% increase (20 out of 80), not 20% (20 out of 100).
- **Stopping at the first pattern.** If +4 fits the first three numbers but not the fourth, the rule is something else. Check every term.
- **Long division when estimation would do.** If the options are 30, 45, 75, 100 and 150, you rarely need an exact sum to choose.
- **Copying a figure wrong from a table.** Point at the row and column you need. It costs a second and saves an item.
- **Freezing on one hard item.** A number series you cannot crack in 20 seconds is a guess and move on. PI scores only correct answers, so a guess costs nothing.

## Timing

Series and simple percentages can take under 10 seconds once you know the patterns. Word problems and tables take longer, often 20 seconds or more. Because this is the largest area, it is where faster arithmetic buys you the most extra questions. See the [timing guide](../timing.md) for a full pacing plan.

## Practice items

These 15 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the PI Cognitive Assessment or from PI's sample questions. Try them in **3 minutes 30 seconds**, then open the explanations.

### Number series

**Item 1.** What comes next? 3, 7, 11, 15, ?
A) 17  B) 18  C) 19  D) 21  E) 23

**Item 2.** What comes next? 2, 6, 18, 54, ?
A) 72  B) 108  C) 148  D) 162  E) 216

**Item 3.** What comes next? 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ?
A) 30  B) 34  C) 36  D) 42  E) 49

**Item 4.** What comes next? 5, 6, 8, 11, 15, ?
A) 18  B) 19  C) 20  D) 21  E) 22

**Item 5.** What comes next? 40, 36, 33, 31, ?
A) 27  B) 28  C) 29  D) 30  E) 31

**Item 6.** What comes next? 2, 9, 4, 8, 6, 7, ?
A) 5  B) 6  C) 7  D) 8  E) 10

### Percentages, ratios and rates

**Item 7.** A jacket priced at 80 is reduced by 25%. What is the new price?
A) 55  B) 60  C) 64  D) 65  E) 70

**Item 8.** A recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of milk. How much milk goes with 9 cups of flour?
A) 4 cups  B) 5 cups  C) 6 cups  D) 7 cups  E) 8 cups

**Item 9.** A machine fills 120 bottles in 8 minutes. At the same rate, how many bottles does it fill in 30 minutes?
A) 360  B) 400  C) 420  D) 450  E) 480

**Item 10.** Four test scores are 70, 85, 90 and 75. What is the average?
A) 78  B) 79  C) 80  D) 81  E) 82

**Item 11.** Which is the largest?
A) 3/5  B) 5/8  C) 2/3  D) 7/12  E) 0.6

**Item 12.** Monthly sales rose from 200 to 250. What is the percentage increase?
A) 20%  B) 25%  C) 30%  D) 40%  E) 50%

**Item 13.** A car travels at 60 km per hour for 2.5 hours. How far does it go?
A) 120 km  B) 135 km  C) 140 km  D) 150 km  E) 165 km

### Tables and two-step problems

**Item 14.** Sales over three days:

| | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team A | 12 | 15 | 9 |
| Team B | 10 | 14 | 16 |

Over the three days, which team sold more, and by how many?
A) Team A by 2  B) Team A by 4  C) Team B by 2  D) Team B by 4  E) They sold the same

**Item 15.** A company has 250 staff. 30% of them work remotely. Of the remote workers, 40% are in sales. How many remote workers are in sales?
A) 25  B) 30  C) 35  D) 40  E) 75

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 15</summary>

**Item 1: C) 19.** The step is +4 each time: 15 + 4 = 19.

**Item 2: D) 162.** Each number is three times the one before: 54 × 3 = 162. *Why not B:* 108 is 54 × 2, but the rule is ×3 all the way through.

**Item 3: C) 36.** These are the square numbers 1², 2², 3², 4², 5², so the next is 6² = 36.

**Item 4: C) 20.** The steps grow by one: +1, +2, +3, +4, so the next step is +5: 15 + 5 = 20.

**Item 5: D) 30.** The steps shrink by one: −4, −3, −2, so the next is −1: 31 − 1 = 30.

**Item 6: D) 8.** Two series are interleaved. Odd positions go 2, 4, 6, and even positions go 9, 8, 7. The seventh number is an odd position, so it continues 2, 4, 6 with 8. *Why not B:* 6 would be the next even-position number, which comes after the 7.

**Item 7: B) 60.** 25% of 80 is a quarter of 80, which is 20. 80 − 20 = 60.

**Item 8: C) 6 cups.** 9 cups of flour is three times 3 cups, so the milk is three times 2 cups: 6 cups.

**Item 9: D) 450.** 120 ÷ 8 = 15 bottles a minute. 15 × 30 = 450.

**Item 10: C) 80.** 70 + 85 + 90 + 75 = 320, and 320 ÷ 4 = 80.

**Item 11: C) 2/3.** As decimals: 3/5 = 0.6, 5/8 = 0.625, 2/3 ≈ 0.667, 7/12 ≈ 0.583, and 0.6. The largest is 2/3.

**Item 12: B) 25%.** The rise is 50. Percentage increase is the rise divided by the starting value: 50 ÷ 200 = 0.25 = 25%. *Why not A:* 20% divides by the new value, 250, which is the classic slip.

**Item 13: D) 150 km.** Distance is speed × time: 60 × 2.5 = 150.

**Item 14: D) Team B by 4.** Team A: 12 + 15 + 9 = 36. Team B: 10 + 14 + 16 = 40. Team B sold 4 more.

**Item 15: B) 30.** 30% of 250 = 75 remote workers. 40% of 75 = 30. *Why not E:* 75 is the number of remote workers, the first step only.

</details>

## Score yourself

- **13 to 15:** numbers are a strength. Push for speed: aim to do the series items in under 10 seconds each.
- **9 to 12:** if series cost you most, learn the series checklist above until you can recite it. If word problems did, practise the "find one first" step.
- **8 or fewer:** spend ten minutes a day on mental arithmetic (times tables to 12, quarters and tenths of round numbers) for a week, then redo these items.

Practice scores are a guide to where to work, not a prediction of your PI score.
