# Verbal reasoning

**In one line:** short word and sentence puzzles, answered in seconds, that check how well you understand and reason with written information.

Verbal reasoning is one of the three content areas of the PI Cognitive Assessment. You can meet up to 16 verbal questions in a single test, mixed in with the numerical and abstract ones.

## What it measures

PI describes this area as the ability to understand and analyse written information and comprehend complex ideas. It is not a vocabulary exam or a spelling test. The words are everyday words, and PI says its verbal items avoid idioms so they work across cultures and translations.

What the questions really reward is precision under time pressure: seeing the exact relationship between two words, or the exact thing a pair of sentences proves.

## How the questions look

PI does not publish a list of verbal question types. The formats below are the ones we use for practice in these notes. They cover the skills PI names, and they are the kind of short items you can answer in about 14 seconds.

| Format | What you do |
|---|---|
| Word relationships (analogies) | Find the link between two words, then the word that completes a second pair the same way |
| Same and opposite meaning | Pick the word closest to, or furthest from, a given word |
| Odd one out | Find the word that does not share what the others have in common |
| Statement logic | Treat two short statements as true and decide whether a third is true, false or uncertain |
| Ordering | Work out an order (taller, earlier, cheaper) from a few comparisons |
| Sentence completion | Choose the word that makes the sentence make sense |

The official PI sample questions, if your employer includes them, show the real look and feel. Check your invitation email.

## A method that works

1. **Name the relationship in a short sentence.** "A brush is used to paint." Then apply the same sentence to the second pair: "A needle is used to ___." If the sentence is vague ("they are both in an office"), make it tighter.
2. **For meaning questions, say the word in a simple sentence.** "The room was spacious" means there was plenty of room. Now look for the word that means "cramped" (opposite) or "roomy" (same).
3. **For odd one out, find the group first.** Name what four of them share. The answer is the one left over. If two words seem to fit the "odd" slot, your group is too loose.
4. **For statement logic, use only what is written.** Picture the statements as rules that cannot be broken. Ask: must it be true, must it be false, or could it go either way?
5. **For ordering, write a quick line.** "A > B > C, D < C" on scrap paper takes three seconds and stops you mixing up names.
6. **Pick, then move.** On a 12-minute test there is no time for a second full reading. If you have narrowed it to two, choose and go.

## Traps that cost points

- **Reversing the pair.** "Acorn is to oak" runs from young to grown. An option that runs the other way ("sheep is to lamb") looks related but goes backwards.
- **Choosing a word that is related, but in the wrong way.** "Wet" is linked to "umbrella", but an umbrella is used to keep you dry. Linked is not the same as the right link.
- **Bringing in outside facts in statement logic.** If the statements say every train on a line is electric, that is the rule, whatever you know about real railways.
- **Treating "some" as "all".** "Some nurses on the ward are trainers" tells you nothing certain about any single nurse.
- **Missing a negative.** "Opposite of", "not", "no" and "except" change the answer. Read the question stem before the options.
- **Spending 40 seconds on one word you do not know.** If a word is unfamiliar, eliminate what you can and guess. PI scores only correct answers, so a guess cannot lose you anything.

## Timing

Most verbal items here are short enough for 10 to 15 seconds. Statement logic and ordering items take a little longer because you have to hold two or three facts in your head. Bank time on the quick analogies and meaning questions, so you can afford 20 seconds on a logic item when you need it. The [timing guide](../timing.md) has a full pacing plan.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the PI Cognitive Assessment or from PI's sample questions. Try them in **1 minute 10 seconds**, which is the real test's pace, then open the explanations.

For items 4 and 5, treat the statements as true. Is the final sentence **true**, **false** or **uncertain**?

**Item 1.** Seed is to tree as egg is to:
A) nest  B) bird  C) shell  D) feather  E) farm

**Item 2.** Which word means the same as **temporary**?
A) permanent  B) provisional  C) ancient  D) frequent  E) local

**Item 3.** Which word is the odd one out?
A) assist  B) help  C) aid  D) hinder  E) support

**Item 4.** Some drivers on the team are also mechanics. Sam is a driver on the team.
*Sam is a mechanic.*

**Item 5.** If the fire alarm sounds, everyone leaves the building. Today, nobody left the building.
*The fire alarm sounded today.*

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: B) bird.** A seed grows into a tree; an egg develops into a bird. *Why not A:* a nest holds eggs, but an egg does not grow into a nest.

**Item 2: B) provisional.** Temporary and provisional both mean "for now, until something more lasting is in place". *Why not A:* permanent is the opposite.

**Item 3: D) hinder.** Assist, help, aid and support all mean making something easier. Hinder means making it harder.

**Item 4: Uncertain.** Only *some* drivers are mechanics. Sam may or may not be one of them.

**Item 5: False.** If the alarm had sounded today, everyone would have left. Nobody left, so the alarm did not sound. *Why not uncertain:* the rule has no exceptions, so the only way nobody left is that the alarm did not go off.

</details>
