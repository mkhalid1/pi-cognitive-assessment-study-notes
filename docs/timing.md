# Timing and pacing

Twelve minutes for 50 questions is the defining feature of the PI Cognitive Assessment. PI says the time limit is part of what the test measures: without it, the questions would be much easier and the test would no longer show how quickly someone learns. So pacing is not a side issue. It is half the test.

## The budget

| Version | Time | Per question |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 12 minutes | about 14 seconds |
| Extended (time and a half) | 18 minutes | about 22 seconds |
| Extended (double time) | 24 minutes | about 29 seconds |

The extended versions are accommodations you request from the employer before you start. See [the test explained](test-overview.md#extended-time-and-accommodations).

Fourteen seconds is an average, not a rule. Some items take 6 seconds, some take 25. What matters is that a few slow ones do not eat the time for many quick ones.

## A pacing plan for 12 minutes

This is our guidance, built on PI's scoring rule that only correct answers count and wrong answers cost nothing.

| Clock shows (time left) | Aim to have answered about |
|---|---|
| 9:00 | 12 |
| 6:00 | 25 |
| 3:00 | 37 |
| 0:30 | everything you can still reach, then guess the rest |

Three rules make the plan work:

1. **The 20-second rule.** If an item has taken 20 seconds and you still cannot see the answer, pick the most likely option and move on.
2. **Never leave a blank.** A guess has some chance of being right. A blank has none.
3. **Do not chase the plan.** If you are behind at 6:00, speed up on easy items. Do not start skipping items you could get right.

PI's public pages do not say whether you can go back to earlier questions. Read the on-screen instructions, and practise as if every answer is final. That habit is useful either way.

## Where time usually goes

- **Re-reading.** Reading a word problem twice because you skipped the question line the first time. Read the question line first.
- **Series that do not crack.** Trying multiplication, then squares, then something exotic. Use the [series checklist](sections/numerical-reasoning.md#series-checklist) in order, and stop at the 20-second mark.
- **Two-rule shape puzzles.** Finding one rule and then hunting among the options. Name both changes before you look at the options.
- **Second-guessing.** Changing an answer you chose for a good reason. Unless you spot a clear mistake, your first reasoned answer is usually right.
- **Nerves in the first minute.** The first few items feel slow because the format is new. Practising beforehand, with PI's sample questions if your invitation has them, removes most of that.

## Practise against the clock

The practice sets in this repo are 5 items in 1 minute 10 seconds, which is the real pace. Once you can do them comfortably, practise the full length: 50 mixed items in 12 minutes, in one sitting, with no pausing. The [free PI Cognitive practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/pi-cognitive-assessment?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=pi-cognitive-assessment) runs at that length and pace.

After each timed run, note two numbers: how many you answered, and how many of those were right. If the first number is low, work on speed. If the second is low, slow down slightly and check each answer against the question.
