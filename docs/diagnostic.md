# 10-minute diagnostic

Six original items, two from each content area. The real test gives you about 14 seconds a question, so this diagnostic checks your pace as well as your accuracy.

These items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the PI Cognitive Assessment or from PI's sample questions.

## How to take it

1. Get a pen and scrap paper. Set a timer for **1 minute 25 seconds**. That is the real test's pace for six questions.
2. Answer all six in order. If you are stuck, pick your best guess and move on.
3. When the timer ends, mark where you got to. Then finish any items you did not reach, untimed.
4. Check your answers, read every explanation, and use the table at the bottom.

Reading, checking and reviewing takes about 10 minutes in total.

If you already know the question styles, a full timed run tells you more. ## Part A: Verbal reasoning

**D1.** Pen is to write as knife is to:
A) fork  B) cut  C) sharp  D) kitchen  E) steel

**D2.** No cyclists in the club own a car. Priya owns a car.
If both statements are true, is this true, false or uncertain? *Priya is a cyclist in the club.*

## Part B: Numerical reasoning

**D3.** You answer 18 of 24 questions correctly. What percentage is that?
A) 60%  B) 70%  C) 72%  D) 75%  E) 80%

**D4.** What comes next? 7, 10, 9, 12, 11, ?
A) 10  B) 13  C) 14  D) 15  E) 16

## Part C: Abstract reasoning

**D5.** Each row and each column uses each shape once. Which shape replaces the question mark?

```
▲   ■   ●
■   ●   ▲
●   ▲   ?
```

A) ▲  B) ●  C) ■  D) ○

**D6.** What comes next?

```
↖   ↑   ↗   →   ?
```

A) ↓  B) ↘  C) ←  D) ↙

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, D1 to D6</summary>

**D1: B) cut.** A pen is used to write; a knife is used to cut. The relationship is "tool : what it does". *Why not C:* "sharp" describes the knife, it is not what you do with it.

**D2: False.** No club cyclist owns a car, and Priya owns one, so she cannot be a club cyclist. *Why not uncertain:* the first statement rules her out completely.

**D3: D) 75%.** 18 ÷ 24 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. *Quick route:* 6 is a quarter of 24, so 18 is three quarters.

**D4: C) 14.** The steps alternate +3, −1: 7 (+3) 10 (−1) 9 (+3) 12 (−1) 11 (+3) 14.

**D5: C) ■.** The bottom row already has ● and ▲, so it needs ■. The third column already has ● and ▲ too, which confirms it.

**D6: B) ↘.** The arrow turns 45 degrees clockwise each step: up-left, up, up-right, right, then down-right.

</details>

## What your result means

Count your correct answers in each part, and note how many you reached inside the 1 minute 25 seconds.

| Part | Your score | If you scored 0 | If you scored 1 | If you scored 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Verbal | /2 | Start with the [verbal guide](sections/verbal-reasoning.md) | Do its practice items, focus on the statement-logic ones | Light review only |
| B. Numerical | /2 | Start with the [numerical guide](sections/numerical-reasoning.md) | Learn the series checklist | Light review only |
| C. Abstract | /2 | Start with the [abstract guide](sections/abstract-reasoning.md) | Practise naming the rule out loud | Light review only |

**If you reached fewer than five items in time,** speed is your main issue, not knowledge. Read [Timing and pacing](timing.md) before anything else.

**If you finished early but made two or more slips,** you are rushing. Slow down by a second or two on each item and check the answer against the question.

Then pick a plan: the [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan) if you have a week, or the [2-day plan](study-plans.md#2-day-plan) if the test is close.

Your diagnostic score is a guide to where to study, not a forecast. PI itself says that results on practice questions are not a prediction of your real score.
