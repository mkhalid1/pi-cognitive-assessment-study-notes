# The PI Cognitive Assessment explained

**In one line:** 50 multiple-choice questions on words, numbers and shapes, in 12 minutes, scored on how many you get right.

The PI Cognitive Assessment is The Predictive Index's test of general cognitive ability for hiring. Employers send it to candidates, usually alongside the PI Behavioral Assessment, to see how quickly someone is likely to pick up a new job. Everything on this page comes from The Predictive Index's own pages, listed in [Sources](sources.md).

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | The Predictive Index (PI) |
| Questions | 50 multiple-choice questions |
| Time | 12 minutes. Extended-time versions of 18 and 24 minutes exist as accommodations. |
| Content | Verbal reasoning, numerical reasoning, abstract reasoning |
| Maximum per area | 16 verbal, 18 numerical, 16 abstract |
| Score | A scaled score from 100 to 450, based only on the number of correct answers |
| Average | PI says the general population average is 250 |
| Pass mark | None. Each employer sets a target score for the role. |
| Question pool | Each candidate gets a different set of questions from a larger pool |
| Prior knowledge | None needed. PI says it requires no job knowledge, formal education or test preparation. |

## What it measures

PI describes the test as a measure of a person's capacity to learn, adapt and grasp new concepts at work. Its own wording is that the assessment "indicates how fast an individual can be expected to acquire new knowledge."

Just as useful is what PI says it does **not** measure: IQ, knowledge you already have (such as job-specific knowledge), your behaviours, or your willingness to make an effort to learn. So there is nothing to revise in the school sense. What you can train is familiarity with the question styles and a calm, fast pace.

## The three content areas

| Area | What PI says it covers | Up to | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbal reasoning | Understanding and analysing written information and complex ideas | 16 questions | [Verbal reasoning](sections/verbal-reasoning.md) |
| Numerical reasoning | Working with numbers, interpreting data, solving quantitative problems quickly and accurately | 18 questions | [Numerical reasoning](sections/numerical-reasoning.md) |
| Abstract reasoning | Identifying patterns and solving novel problems using logic | 16 questions | [Abstract reasoning](sections/abstract-reasoning.md) |

The three areas are mixed into one test. PI treats the result as a single measure of general cognitive ability. It reports sub-scores for each area but says they should not be used for any decision. More on that in [How scoring works](scoring.md).

## How the test is built

- **A different test for everyone.** PI uses a linear-on-the-fly (LOFT) engine. It picks 50 questions from a larger pool so that each version has equivalent content and difficulty, and so that the same person does not get the exact same test twice.
- **Built for work, not school.** PI says it developed the test in 2010 for the workplace. Doctorate-level consultants wrote the original pool of 315 items, which then went through accuracy and cultural reviews.
- **Culturally neutral by design.** PI says verbal items avoid idioms, and abstract items avoid Latin letters in their patterns and rotations, so the test works across countries and translations.
- **Timed on purpose.** PI explains that speed is part of the ability it measures. Without the time limit the questions would be much easier, and the test would no longer show how quickly someone learns.
- **Security.** PI says the test blurs the screen and blocks copy and paste if you navigate away. The question pool and LOFT design mean shared "real questions" are of little use.

## Who uses it and when

PI says the assessment is meant for job candidates, or existing employees applying for a new position with different demands. It is not meant as a development tool for people already in a role. In practice you usually meet it after applying for a job, as an email link from the employer, often next to the [PI Behavioral Assessment](with-the-behavioral-assessment.md).

PI recommends that employers treat the score as one data point among several, alongside the CV, interviews and the behavioural results.

## Extended time and accommodations

If you have a disability that affects timed tests, you can ask the employer for an accommodation **before** you start. PI offers 18-minute (time and a half) and 24-minute (double time) versions, scored on the same scale as the 12-minute version. PI notes that in the United States the extra time is normally specified by a medical professional, and that employers should tell candidates they can ask. Other help, such as a quiet room or pencil and paper, can also be arranged by the employer.

## Languages

PI's language list for the Cognitive Assessment includes more than 50 languages, from Afrikaans to Zulu. If English is not your strongest language, ask the employer whether you can take it in another one.

## Practise the real format

PI has official sample questions. Employers can include the link in the invitation, so check your email first. PI says the sample questions are new questions, not taken from the real pool, and that your result on them is not a prediction of your real score. They are there so the format does not come as a shock.
