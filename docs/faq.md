# FAQ

Answers to the questions people most often search for about the PI Cognitive Assessment. The questions come from Google's "People also ask" results; the answers come from PI's own pages, listed in [Sources](sources.md).

## What is a PI assessment?

"PI assessment" usually means one of the two tests from The Predictive Index. The **PI Cognitive Assessment** is a 12-minute, 50-question test of general cognitive ability. The **PI Behavioral Assessment** is an untimed adjective checklist about your workplace drives. Employers often send both. See [taking both assessments](with-the-behavioral-assessment.md).

## What is the Predictive Index Cognitive Assessment?

A timed workplace test that PI says measures a person's capacity to learn, adapt and grasp new concepts. It has 50 multiple-choice questions on verbal, numerical and abstract reasoning. PI says it does not measure IQ or knowledge you already have. Full details in [the test explained](test-overview.md).

## What is the 12-minute, 50-question cognitive test?

Usually the PI Cognitive Assessment: 50 questions, 12 minutes, three content areas. Other publishers also make short timed ability tests, so check the publisher's name in your invitation before you prepare.

## How long does the Predictive Index test take?

The cognitive assessment is 12 minutes, or 18 or 24 minutes for candidates given extended time as an accommodation. The behavioural assessment is untimed and PI says it takes about six minutes on average. Allow a little extra for reading the instructions.

## Is the PI Cognitive Assessment hard?

The questions themselves are not advanced. PI says the test needs no formal education or job knowledge. What makes it hard is the pace: about 14 seconds a question. PI says the time limit is deliberate, because speed is part of the ability it measures. Practice makes the formats familiar, which is where most people find time. Start with the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md).

## What is a good score on a PI cognitive test?

It depends on the job. Each employer sets a target score for the role, and your score is compared with that target. PI says the general population average is 250 on a scale of 100 to 450, and scoring above the target carries no penalty. PI does not publish targets for particular jobs. See [How scoring works](scoring.md#what-counts-as-a-good-score).

## What are the scoring methods for the Predictive Index test?

For the cognitive assessment, the score is based only on the number of correct answers, converted to a scaled score from 100 to 450. Wrong answers and unanswered questions are not penalised beyond not earning a point. The employer compares your scaled score with the job target as a match score. The behavioural assessment is scored completely differently, with no right answers. Details in [How scoring works](scoring.md).

## What are the different types of Predictive Index tests?

For candidates, the two you will meet are the PI Cognitive Assessment and the PI Behavioral Assessment. PI also has a Job Assessment, which employers fill in to set the targets for a role. You do not take that one as a candidate.

## What is the Predictive Index test used for?

Hiring, mainly. PI says the cognitive assessment is meant for candidates, or existing employees applying for a role with different demands, and not as a development tool. The behavioural assessment is used for hiring and also for coaching and team building after someone joins.

## How do I prepare for the PI Cognitive Assessment?

Learn the three question styles, practise at the real pace, and arrive rested. A short plan: take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md), work through your weakest [section guide](sections/verbal-reasoning.md), do one full timed run, then follow the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). The [7-day and 2-day plans](study-plans.md) lay it out day by day.

## How do I "crack" the PI cognitive test?

There is no trick that beats it. PI builds each test from a large question pool, so recalled questions will not match yours, and it uses screen blurring and copy-paste blocking if you leave the test window. What works is legitimate: knowing the formats, knowing the pacing rules (guess rather than leave blanks, move on after about 20 seconds) and practising until the styles feel familiar.

## Can I use a calculator?

PI's public pages do not say. Follow the instructions in your invitation and on the test's first screen. The arithmetic is designed to be quick, so practise doing it in your head or with a short note on paper.

## Can I retake the PI Cognitive Assessment?

That is up to the employer. PI says each company should set its own policy, and some employers give the test twice. Because of the LOFT design, a retake gives you a different set of questions, not the same test again.

## Do I get to see my score?

Usually not. PI recommends that employers keep cognitive scores confidential and describe results in terms of fit to the role. PI does not release individual questions or answers. You can ask the employer for feedback.

## Are there official PI sample questions?

Yes. PI has a set of sample questions that employers can include in the invitation, so check your email. PI says they are unique questions, not taken from the real pool, and that you see which you got right but not a score. PI also says your result on them does not predict your real score.

## Can I get extra time?

If you have a disability that affects timed tests, ask the employer before you start. PI offers 18-minute and 24-minute versions, scored on the same scale as the standard test. See [extended time and accommodations](test-overview.md#extended-time-and-accommodations).

## Can I take it in another language?

Often, yes. PI lists more than 50 languages for the Cognitive Assessment. Ask the employer which ones they can send.

## Is the PI Cognitive Assessment an IQ test?

PI says no. It describes the test as a measure of general cognitive ability for the workplace, focused on how quickly someone can learn, and says it does not measure IQ or acquired knowledge.

## Is this the cognitive test where you remember five words?

No. Tests that ask you to remember and recall a short list of words are memory screening tests used in health care. The PI Cognitive Assessment is a timed reasoning test for job applicants, with no memory task.

## Where can I practise for free?

Three places: the [practice items in this repo](sections/verbal-reasoning.md#practice-items), PI's own sample questions if your invitation includes the link, and the [free 50-question PI practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/pi-cognitive-assessment?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=ngp6xf&utm_content=pi-cognitive-assessment), which runs at the real length and pace.
